Research / Papers

Current single-authored research

These projects examine political rhetoric, AI-mediated presence, and family communication through computational measurement, experimental design, and causal inference.

Accepted Political Communication

Exclusionary Populism in the U.S.: From Party-wide Patterns to Trump’s Rhetoric

International Association for Media and Communication Research (IAMCR) 2026, Political Communication Research Section (POL); selected as Panel Chair

Galway, Ireland · Jun. – Jul. 2026

This project traces exclusionary populist rhetoric in U.S. presidential campaign speeches using a party-month panel from 1952 to 2024, the Annenberg Presidential Database, LIWC-based text measures, and a Python workflow for text processing, longitudinal modeling, and visualization.

Exclusionary Populism Index LIWC measurement Panel modeling Python
Accepted AI Ethics

When the Dead Seem Present: Perceived Realism, Social Presence, and Psychological Mechanisms in AI-based Digital Resurrection

IAMCR 2026, Ethics of Society and Ethics of Communication Working Group (ETH)

Galway, Ireland · Jun. – Jul. 2026

The study examines AI-based digital resurrection through perceived realism, social presence, emotional authenticity and comfort, uncanny discomfort, and behavioral intention in a preregistered between-subjects online survey experiment in Qualtrics with a three-condition technical fidelity manipulation.

Survey experiment Moderated parallel mediation Robustness checks Qualtrics
Submitted Text Analysis

Speaking as the People, the Protector, or the Provocateur: Rhetorical Persona Types in U.S. Presidential Campaign Speeches (1952–2024)

Submitted to National Communication Association (NCA) 2026, Political Communication Division

This paper identifies recurring rhetorical personae across U.S. presidential campaign speeches by combining a populism continuum, people-centered language, anti-elite appeals, policy-oriented discourse, threat-based rhetoric, LIWC-based lexical indicators, and latent profile analysis.

Latent profile analysis Rhetorical signatures LIWC indicators Party and period variation
Submitted Causal Inference

When Home Moves Away: The Causal Effect of Parental Rural-to-Urban Migration on Rural-Origin Children’s Intergenerational Ties in China

Submitted to National Communication Association (NCA) 2026, Family Communication Division

Using China Family Panel Studies data, this project studies left-behind, co-migrant, and non-migrant rural-origin children through parent-child communication, emotional attention, parental monitoring, conflict, propensity-score matching, and difference-in-differences.

CFPS Propensity-score methods Difference-in-differences Migration studies
