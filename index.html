Conference Papers

Speaking as the People, the Protector, or the Provocateur: Rhetorical Persona Types in U.S. Presidential Campaign Speeches (1952–2024)

Single-authored

Accepted for presentation at the 112th Annual Convention of the National Communication Association (NCA), Political Communication Division.

New Orleans, Louisiana, United States · November 2026

Develops a person-centered framework for recurring multidimensional rhetorical personae. A latent profile analysis of full-text campaign speeches identifies speech-level personae, candidate-level rhetorical signatures, and differences across parties, periods, and candidates.

When Home Moves Away: The Causal Effect of Parental Rural-to-Urban Migration on Rural-Origin Children’s Intergenerational Ties in China

Single-authored

Accepted for presentation at the 112th Annual Convention of the National Communication Association (NCA), Family Communication Division.

New Orleans, Louisiana, United States · November 2026

Reframes migration as a reorganization of the family communication environment. A propensity-score-weighted difference-in-differences analysis of CFPS panel data compares communication, emotional attention, monitoring, and conflict across family migration arrangements.

Exclusionary Populism in the U.S.: From Party-Wide Patterns to Trump’s Rhetoric

Single-authored

Presented at IAMCR 2026, Political Communication Research Section (POL); served as Panel Chair.

Galway, Ireland · June–July 2026

Constructs a party-month panel of campaign speeches and a LIWC-based Exclusionary Populism Index. The results locate Trump’s rhetoric within broader Republican exclusionary trends and examine how prior elite and people-centered rhetoric predict the they–we gap.

When the Dead Seem Present: Perceived Realism, Social Presence, and Psychological Mechanisms in AI-Based Digital Resurrection

Single-authored

Presented at IAMCR 2026, Ethics of Society and Ethics of Communication Working Group (ETH).

Galway, Ireland · June–July 2026

Develops a mechanism model linking perceived realism to willingness to use digital resurrection through emotional comfort and uncanny discomfort, conditional on social presence. The preregistered experiment manipulates technical fidelity across three conditions.
